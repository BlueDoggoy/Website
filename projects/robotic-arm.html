6-DOF rehabilitative robotic arm

A cable-driven platform for exploring upper-limb assistance during daily tasks, developed from system-level CAD through dynamic motion analysis.

Designing for useful motion

Six degrees of freedom give the mechanism the articulation needed to explore representative upper-limb trajectories. Cable transmission moves actuation away from selected joints, helping manage mass across the moving links while preserving a useful range of motion.

Coordinating the mechanical system

I led more than ten students working in Onshape and coordinated the shared assembly across independently developed subsystems. Mounting patterns, pulley paths, structural interfaces, and motion envelopes were treated as system-level requirements so each contribution would integrate cleanly.

Validating before fabrication

The complete assembly was brought into MSC Adams to examine joint motion, component interaction, and representative assistance trajectories. This provided an early dynamic check before committing the design to a physical build.
