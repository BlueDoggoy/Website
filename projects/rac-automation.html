Integrating instruments into automated biology

Mechanical design, controlled documentation, hardware bring-up, motion teaching, and fault isolation for laboratory automation platforms.

This public summary intentionally omits proprietary drawings, dimensions, internal procedures, and unreleased equipment details.

The integration challenge

Adding a laboratory instrument to an automated platform is an interface problem across mechanical, electrical, controls, and software disciplines. The instrument needs a stable enclosure, compatible utilities, reliable labware access, and robot motion paths that will not introduce collisions or dropped material.

Mechanical ownership

I led an enclosure redesign that made room for laboratory equipment that could not fit within the existing platform. I also converted a vendor drawing package into 13 released, PLM-compliant internal drawings, helping bring design control and future revisions in-house.

Beyond CAD, I managed multilevel bills of materials, item lifecycles, and component inventory in Aligni. Connecting design releases with current inventory created a clearer path from an approved assembly to a buildable hardware kit.

From installation to reliable motion

I assembled automation hardware and instrument interfaces, then supported systems from physical installation through Python driver integration. I calibrated six-axis robot trajectories for precise labware handling and worked across mechanical and controls boundaries to prevent collisions and dropped labware.

System-level debugging

Automation failures rarely stay within one discipline. I investigated hardware faults, measured inrush current and operating loads, and used those results to strengthen Factory Acceptance Testing power criteria. This work reinforced the importance of debugging the complete system rather than treating CAD, electrical behavior, and software operation as separate problems.
